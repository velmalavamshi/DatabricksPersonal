# Regulated, Customer-Facing, and Sensitive Content — Extended Guidance

The non-negotiable rules (off-label handling, confidentiality, the SME/regulatory sign-off
requirement) are in SKILL.md's Terminology and Tone sections and apply everywhere — this file
is the extended review-routing and proofing detail, not a second copy of those rules.

## Who reviews what

- **SME (subject-matter expert) review**: technical/clinical accuracy of claims about a
  product's function, performance, or indicated use.
- **Regulatory review**: anything that could read as an indication, efficacy, or safety claim
  — especially content that names a specific product alongside a clinical outcome.
- **Legal review**: font/asset licensing questions (see `references/typography.md`), and
  anything involving a real person's employment status or performance record.
- **BrandManagement@Hologic.com**: logo/lock-up questions not covered in SKILL.md or
  `terminology.md` — new product logos, ambiguous ® placement, unconfirmed trademark symbols.

Genie or Claude can draft customer-facing, clinical, or regulated content — a human always
owns final sign-off before it ships. Say so explicitly in the response rather than implying
the draft is ready to publish as-is.

## Off-label content — decision flow

1. Does the content describe a product being used, or performing, outside its cleared/approved
   indication?
2. If yes: don't rewrite around it to make it sound compliant. Either
   - **cut the mention** and tell the user it was removed and why, or
   - **leave it in and flag it** for Regulatory review before anything ships.
3. A rewrite that removes the explicit "off-label" language but keeps the product name next to
   the outcome is still a claim — it hasn't been made compliant, just less obviously flagged.
   Treat this the same as leaving it unflagged.

## Sensitive/confidential content

Applies to anything this skill touches, not just clinical/regulated writing — HR-sensitive
notes, exec-only markings, personnel performance details, or anything similarly marked in
source material.

- Omit the field, or
- Replace with a neutral placeholder that asserts nothing new ("See program lead for
  details"), or
- Ask the user how they want it handled.

**Never invent a specific, plausible-sounding replacement fact.** A fabricated detail about a
real person (e.g., turning "possible PIP" into "onboarding still in progress") is worse than
leaving the original sensitive line untouched, because the reader has no way to know the
replacement was invented.

## Proofing checklist (corporate-level collateral)

Before leadership review: spell check/grammar complete · AP + Hologic word-list exceptions
applied · proper nouns and titles validated · Divisional Marcom/Regulatory/Legal reviewed where
necessary · boilerplate and references validated · trademarks verified · read aloud and
back-to-front · second-person proofread (someone other than the author) · no off-label claims.

**Press releases need the dedicated Press Release Review Checklist, not this general one** —
ask if that checklist isn't already in hand; don't substitute this list for it.
